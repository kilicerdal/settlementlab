# V4.3 — investigate, recover and prove the result

Status: implementation candidate `4.3.0.dev1`. V4.2 remains the published release. Website source and deployment are unchanged.

V4.3 turns individual framework controls into a reproducible investigation. Follow one obligation through an actual worker interruption, partial execution, delayed and discrepant statements, evidence-backed resolution and separately authorized compensation. Every step retains its original evidence.

## One-command demonstration

From a fresh checkout, with Python 3.9+:

```sh
python3 scripts/incident_quickstart.py
```

No package installation, API key, cloud account, model download or paid service is required. The command creates a unique output directory, starts a real child worker, deliberately exits it at the after-claim checkpoint, recovers its expired lease, verifies the incident and starts the local workbench at `http://127.0.0.1:8086/investigation`. Stop with Ctrl+C. The database and evidence remain in the printed output directory.

For a noninteractive run, use `python3 scripts/incident_quickstart.py --no-serve`. Installed wheels also provide `settlementlab-incident --exercise --serve --output FRESH_DIRECTORY`. An existing incident database is never silently overwritten.

## The reproducible incident

1. Create and independently approve a synthetic 100-unit obligation at 4,275 cents per unit.
2. Accept a durable 40-unit execution. A real worker exits 73 after claiming the job, before posting.
3. A recovery worker fences the expired attempt and commits exactly one execution, processing receipt and outbox record. The original attempt remains visible.
4. Import a delayed zero-execution statement, then a 40-unit statement whose amount is wrong by one cent. Retain both discrepancies and their original comparison snapshots.
5. Export `investigate.zip`, showing the unresolved incident.
6. Import corrected evidence for 40 units / 171,000 cents and resolve both cases against it.
7. A maker requests compensation of the original execution. A different checker approves exact inverse entries. Gross execution remains 40, reversed quantity becomes 40 and net execution becomes zero.
8. Import a new zero-net statement. Export `resolved.zip`, including the full chain of evidence.

A recovered job does not establish why its original worker stopped. The demonstration explicitly records its injected checkpoint; ordinary incident observations make no unsupported root-cause claim.

## Investigation and export

The investigation screen links accepted revisions, manager submissions, approval decisions, durable jobs, attempts, processing receipts, outbox events, journal batches, imported statements, reconciliation cases and correction decisions. Filters separate worker, financial, statement and approval evidence. Selecting an event shows its complete record and its correlation links.

Each snapshot is assembled in one SQLite transaction. Timeline ordering uses actual normalized UTC timestamps; the simulated business date is reported separately. At most 5,000 timeline nodes are returned; oversized investigations fail explicitly rather than silently dropping history.

`GET /api/trades/{id}/investigation` returns the graph. `GET /api/trades/{id}/incident-export` returns a ZIP containing:

- `index.html`: offline escaped timeline and financial summary.
- `incident.json`: complete captured records, comparisons, journal lines and correlations.
- `manifest.json`: framework version, source commit/dirty state when available, Python version, trade version and content checksums.

Verify a ZIP without running the service:

```sh
settlementlab-incident --verify PATH_TO_EXPORT.zip
```

Checksums detect changed content; they do not authenticate the author, prove economic correctness or constitute independent review. An installed wheel reports package provenance without guessing the commit of an unrelated working directory. Exports contain full related synthetic statement snapshots, potentially including other obligations from the same full statement. Do not use this demonstration with real personal or financial data. JSON export is bounded at 16 MiB.

## Controlled reversal accounting

A reversal targets one complete original `DVP_PARTIAL` or `DVP_SETTLEMENT` batch belonging to the obligation. Funding, arbitrary batches, already compensated batches and another trade's execution cannot be reversed through this API.

| Action | Identity | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `request-reversal` | Maker | Reason, current version and idempotency key required. Fence active work and enter `CORRECTION_PENDING`. |
| `approve-reversal` | Different checker | Verify original economics, then atomically commit inverse journal entries, linked correction decision, version change and audit evidence. |
| `reject-reversal` | Different checker | Retain rejection and restore the prior state. Fenced jobs remain fenced and require reviewed requeue or a new command. |

Requests use `{version, original_batch, reason}` and `Idempotency-Key`. Decisions use `{version, reversal_id, reason}`. Exact request/decision replay does not post again; conflicting replays and stale versions are rejected. Financial capacity must support the inverse transfer. Insufficient capacity or a transaction failure leaves the request pending and posts nothing.

Original execution rows and gross settlement receipts are immutable. The trade exposes `settled_quantity`, `reversed_quantity`, `net_settled_quantity` and their cent amounts. Full compensation of all posted quantity produces `REVERSED`; otherwise the trade enters `CORRECTION_REVIEW`. Both close ordinary execution on that obligation. Further original batches may be separately reviewed for compensation. Reopening or replacing a corrected obligation is outside this release.

The newer `/api/reconciliation` workflow compares full statements with **net executed obligations**, including zero-net receipt rows. Original imported snapshots and earlier resolutions remain historical. The legacy `/api/reconcile` contract continues to inspect gross receipts for compatibility; do not use it to assert post-correction net reconciliation.

The existing durable observer records settlement delivery, not a live comprehensive correction projection. Correction evidence and current financial truth are in the investigation and journal; a historical delivered settlement event is not overwritten to imply it included a later reversal.

## Independent reviewer exercise

The quick start also creates a separate `reviewer-exercise/` directory. Its deliberately defective database contains a compensating cash transfer that is one cent too small, while conservation and the balance projection still pass. The independent compensation oracle detects the economic error against separately retained accepted terms.

Give an outside engineer only `REVIEWER_WORKSHEET.md` and `reviewer-exercise.zip` initially. Keep the separate answer key for evaluation. The original incident database is unchanged. The exercise ZIP has valid content checksums, demonstrating why checksums alone cannot prove accounting correctness.

Actual independent human review is **pending**. The generated worksheet, oracle and AI evaluation do not constitute an outside review. Record reviewer identity, source commit, findings and dispositions in a real review.

## Optional AI investigator

`POST /api/trades/{id}/investigate-ai` requires the checker demo role and `{version}`. An already configured local Ollama model receives minimized observation codes and evidence IDs rather than raw statement rows, account identities or free-form operator reasons. It has no tools or financial mutation interface.

Structured claims must use supplied codes and their exact supporting citation IDs. Empty evidence requires abstention. Uncertainty, size bounds, duplicate fields, known unsafe language and version checks are enforced. A concurrent trade change can make a completed response stale; the response retains its analyzed version and the UI identifies it.

[AI evaluation and actual local samples](V4_3_AI.md) distinguish offline contract fixtures from live model behavior. Guardrails cannot prove arbitrary prose is semantically true.
